Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐ hard-ish · Camp verdict: ⚠️ rarely worth it · Do instead: Homing Enemies, Enemy States, RayCast2D

#What it is

An enemy that finds its way around walls to reach you, instead of walking straight at you and getting stuck on a corner. Godot provides NavigationRegion2D (the walkable area) and NavigationAgent2D (the thing that follows a computed path).

#Why it's usually not worth it in five days

It's not conceptually hard — it's fiddly. You have to bake a navigation mesh, keep it in sync with your TileMapLayer whenever the level changes, and get the agent's radius and avoidance settings right or enemies jitter, hug walls, or refuse to move. Every one of those is a debugging session, and the failure mode is a silent "enemy stands still."

The real question is whether your game needs it. In a single-screen arcade game — which is most camp games — a straight-line chaser is not just adequate, it's correct. Pac-Man's ghosts use hand-written rules, not pathfinding.

#Cheaper answers that usually work

SituationDo this
Open arena, few wallsHoming Enemies — steer straight at the player
Enemy snags on cornersAdd a RayCast2D ahead; if blocked, slide along the wall
Platformer enemyPatrol Enemies + a ledge raycast. Enemies rarely need to reach you
Grid/puzzle gameStep one tile toward the player — Grid-Based Movement has this in five lines
Enemy should lose youEnemy States with a give-up distance — often reads smarter than perfect tracking

The "slide along the wall when blocked" trick covers most of what people want pathfinding for:

func do_chase(_delta):
	var dir = (player.global_position - global_position).normalized()
	$Forward.target_position = dir * 30
	$Forward.force_raycast_update()
	if $Forward.is_colliding():
		dir = dir.rotated(PI / 2)      # blocked -- try going around
	velocity = dir * chase_speed
💡Perfect tracking isn't fun

An enemy that always finds the optimal route is harder to escape but usually less enjoyable to play against. Predictable, readable enemy movement is better design for a small game. This is a real design point, not just a scope excuse.

#If a student wants it after camp

Genuinely a good next-step project, and much more pleasant when you're not on a deadline.

  1. Add a NavigationRegion2D, draw or bake the walkable polygon.
  2. Add a NavigationAgent2D to the enemy.
  3. Each frame: agent.target_position = player.global_position, then move toward agent.get_next_path_position().
  • Godot docs → Navigation (2D section)
  • Search godot 4 navigation2d tilemap for baking nav from tiles
  • For a grid game, look up A* and Godot's built-in AStarGrid2D — genuinely approachable, and a great introduction to real algorithms

#What to say in the moment

"What's the enemy actually doing wrong? If it's getting stuck on one corner, that's a two-line raycast fix. Full pathfinding is a whole system, and honestly an enemy that's a bit dumb is usually more fun to play against than one that never makes a mistake."
