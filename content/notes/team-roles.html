🏠 Curriculum Map · Used on Day 3 - Design Day, Day 4 - Production Day · 🖨️ Print one card per team.

Everyone on the team has a real job so no one just watches. Roles can rotate day to day — try to make sure each person both codes and does something else during the week.

#The four roles (teams of 3–4)

#🎬 Producer / Lead

Keeps the team on track. Holds the MVP Checklist, watches the clock, and protects scope (The Tiny Game Rules). Speaks at stand-up. Not the boss — the person who keeps everyone unblocked.

#💻 Engineer (1–2 people)

Owns the scripts and scenes. Wires up movement, collisions, spawning, screens. On a team of 4, two engineers split features (e.g. "you do the player, I do the enemies").

#🎨 Artist / Sound

Owns how the game looks and sounds. Finds or makes sprites and audio (Asset Resources, AudioStreamPlayer), picks the color palette, makes the title screen look good. Uses @export variables to tune feel without deep code (GDScript Basics).

#🧪 Playtest Lead

Owns quality. Runs the game constantly, keeps a written bug list, leads the Day 4 playtest exchange, and decides (with the Producer) what's a fix vs. a cut. How: Playtesting.

#Working at the same time

Only one person edits the main project at a time (see Sharing Files on a Team). So split work that doesn't collide:

  • Engineer builds in the project on the main computer.
  • Artist makes/collects art and sound on another machine.
  • Playtest Lead writes the bug list and tests builds.
  • Producer updates the checklist and helps wherever it's thin.

Then bring the pieces together on the main computer.

💡Mixed experience levels

First-timers shine as Artist/Sound or Playtest Lead and still write some code. Put a confident Day-2 coder in the Engineer seat. Everyone codes something during the week.
