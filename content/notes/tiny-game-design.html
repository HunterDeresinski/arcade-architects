🏠 Curriculum Map · Used on Day 3 - Design Day

The crash course in designing a game small enough to finish this week. Read with The Tiny Game Rules.

#Start with the core loop

The core loop is what the player does over and over, every few seconds. Name it before anything else:

  • Pac-Man: move, eat dots, avoid ghosts.
  • Flappy Bird: tap, don't hit a pipe.
  • A coin game: move, grab coins, dodge the bad thing.

If you can't describe what the player does every 5 seconds, you don't have a game yet — you have a setting. Find the verb.

#Constraints make design easier, not harder

A blank page is the enemy. Limits give you something to push against. That's why every game this week takes a constraint:

  • One screen — no scrolling
  • One mechanic — the player really does one thing
  • One button — the whole game is one key

Pick one. It instantly tells you what your game is not, which is most of the work.

#Scope: your idea ÷ 4

Whatever you first imagine, it's about 4× too big for the time you have. Cut it down:

  • 10 levels → 1 level (or endless)
  • A story → a situation
  • 5 enemy types → 1 enemy, maybe 2
  • Multiplayer → single player

You can always add back if you have time. You can't add back time.

#Find the fun fast

Build the core loop first and play it Day 3–4. If grabbing the coin isn't fun with a gray box, no amount of art will save it. If it is fun as boxes, art makes it shine. Prototype before you polish (Paper Prototyping).

#Juice (save for last)

"Juice" = the small feedback that makes actions feel good: a sound on pickup, a little screen shake, the score popping. It's powerful but it's Day 5 polish, never a Day 3–4 priority (Day 5 - Polish and Showcase).

💡A good tiny-game idea fits this template

"It's a game where you [one verb] to [one goal] while [one obstacle], and it ends when [one condition]." If yours fits, you're ready. → One-Sentence Pitch
