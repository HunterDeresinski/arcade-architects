🏠 Curriculum Map

A menu of short breaks to pull from when the room needs an energy shift. Every entry is roughly 10–20 minutes and needs nothing beyond what's already in the room.

The half-day camp has two built-in coffee breaks and doesn't require any of these. Use them when Open Studio runs long, when a group is fried after pitch approval on Day 3 - Design Day, or if you ever run the camp as a full day. Nothing here is scheduled — it's a list to reach for, not a plan to follow.

#Play + Debrief

Play something together, then spend a few minutes writing or talking about it. Closest thing to "curriculum" in this list — same "what's the one idea?" muscle from Days 1–3, exercised on something other than Godot.

  • Play a round of a card game (Uno, Spoons, Skip-Bo, whatever someone brought) → one sentence each: "what was the one rule that made this fun or frustrating?"
  • Watch a 2-minute gameplay clip of something the group likes → discuss the player's one action and the one tension
  • Classic playground game (tag variant, Ninja, Rock-Paper-Scissors tournament) → debrief what rule made it interesting instead of just chaos
  • Trade "best game I played this year" stories in pairs, then each pair reports one out to the group
  • Play a physical/tabletop version of a video game genre (a maze on paper, a stacking game, a matching game) → tie back to "action + tension + failure state" (Tiny Game Design)
  • Mafia / Werewolf (great for a bigger group, needs one confident narrator)
  • Charades, but only game titles or game characters
  • "Guess the mechanic" — describe a game's one rule without naming it, others guess the game

#Screen-Free Design Games

Little design-thinking exercises that don't touch a computer — good energy shift without derailing from the week's actual skills.

  • Design a game using only what's in your pocket/backpack as pieces — 10 minutes, then pitch it in one sentence (One-Sentence Pitch)
  • "Yes, and" game design — one person proposes a rule, the next adds one more, see how far a game idea can go before it collapses
  • Redesign a classic game with one twist ("Tag, but you're all it," "Tic-tac-toe on a bigger board") — playtest it right there
  • Draw a title screen for a game that doesn't exist yet, based on a random word prompt
  • Constraint speed-round: draw a card from Constraint Cards just for fun, 3-minute pitch, no building required
  • Build a tower/structure from cups or cards with a rule twist each round (classic team-builder, easy design-thinking bridge)

#Outside / Active

No debrief needed — the point is leaving the screen and the chairs.

  • Free time outside, no agenda
  • Short walk around the building
  • Gaga ball, kickball, four square, capture the flag, or whatever the outdoor space supports
  • Water balloon or water gun break on a hot day
  • Relay races, sack races, three-legged races
  • Frisbee or a simple catch game
  • Scavenger hunt (nature items, or themed objects)
  • Obstacle course using whatever's on hand (cones, hula hoops, jump ropes)
  • Student-led stretch or warmup (rotate who leads it)

#Quiet / Reflective

For after a heavy stretch — post-lunch, post-pitch-approval, end of day — when the room needs to come down, not up.

  • Journaling prompt: "What's one thing about your game that surprised you today?"
  • Quick sketch break: your character, your title screen, or just doodle
  • Silent reading (bring-a-book day)
  • "One good thing, one hard thing" — everyone writes both, sharing optional
  • Draw your game's world/setting even if it never shows up on screen
  • Write a fake review of your own game, like a store page blurb

#Team / Social / Icebreakers

Good right after a break, or whenever the room's energy is scattered.

  • Two Truths and a Lie (game-themed)
  • Team name / mascot brainstorm for whoever hasn't picked one (Team Roles)
  • Trivia round: guess the game from its one mechanic described out loud
  • Human bingo card themed around games people have played
  • "Would you rather" — game edition (would you rather your game had no sound or no color?)
  • Compliment circle — one specific thing you liked about a teammate's work this week
  • Speed-friending: 2 minutes per pair, then rotate
