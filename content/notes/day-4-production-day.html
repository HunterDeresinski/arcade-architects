#The big idea

MVP = Minimum Viable Product. The smallest version of your game that someone can actually play: they can start, they can win or lose, and they can restart. That's it. Everything else — extra levels, fancy art, particles — is polish, and polish is tomorrow.

#Stand-Up

Every team, 60 seconds. The Producer (Team Roles) speaks. The format is:

  1. What we finished yesterday (one sentence)
  2. What we're building today (one sentence)
  3. What's blocking us (one thing — or "nothing")

Name your blocker out loud so an instructor can help. Blockers that stay silent stay broken.

#Write Your MVP Checklist

On a fresh index card, write the 3–5 things that make your game minimally playable. Tape it to your monitor. This card is your contract with your team.

Good MVP items look like:

  • ✅ Player can move and collide with obstacles
  • ✅ Hitting an obstacle ends the game
  • ✅ A score or timer counts up during play
  • ✅ A "Game Over" screen appears with a restart option
  • ✅ Title screen shows the game name

Bad MVP items:

  • ❌ "Three different enemy types" — that's not MVP, that's Day 6
  • ❌ "Perfect art" — functional art only, today
  • ❌ "Music and sound effects" — nice to have, not must-have

See MVP Checklist for templates and examples.

#Build Sprint

Everyone on the team has a job. Work from your index card down. When you finish one item, cross it off and move to the next.

#What to build first (in order)

  1. Core mechanic — the player action (move, collect, dodge, stack) is working in the game scene.
  2. Fail condition — something can end the game (collision, timer runs out, counter hits zero).
  3. Win condition (if your game has one) — something can end the game in the player's favor.
  4. Restart — pressing a key or clicking a button starts the game over.
  5. Score or feedback — the player knows how they're doing.

Everything else is polish.

#On-demand micro-lessons (side table — throughout the sprint)

Your instructor will run short demos at the side table whenever a topic clusters. Topics that usually come up today — each points to the reference note with the full code and setup steps. By Day 4 the references are your first stop, not the lesson page:

Scene switching — a title screen, and a way to get from it into your game:

func _on_start_button_pressed():
	get_tree().change_scene_to_file("res://Game.tscn")

res:// means "from the project root" — the path has to match your scene's exact name in the FileSystem dock. Full three-screen flow (Title → Game → Game Over) in Scene Switching.

Restart — the one-line version, works for almost every tiny game:

func _on_retry_pressed():
	get_tree().reload_current_scene()

Score labels — same CanvasLayer + Label pattern from Day 1's stretch goals: a Label node under a CanvasLayer, updated with $UI/ScoreLabel.text = "Score: " + str(score). Full version in Score and UI.

A simple moving enemy from scratch — a CharacterBody2D that patrols and turns around at walls. Full scene setup and script in Movement and Input → A simple enemy that moves on its own (patrol).

Collision with Area2D — collecting vs. dying on touch, same Area2D + CollisionShape2D + signal pattern from Day 3's obstacle/pickup build-out. Full pattern in Area2D.

Raise your hand or walk to the side table if you need one of these.

#☕ Break

Step outside if you can. Builds are easier after a real break.

#Continue Build

Keep working from your MVP card. Your goal: every item crossed off before scope check.

⚠️Watch for these traps
  • Adding a new feature before finishing an existing one. Finish what's started first.
  • Everyone working on the same thing. One person per task. Team Roles helps with this.
  • Art before function. A beautiful broken game cannot be showcased. A working ugly game can.

#Scope Check + Instructor Sign-Off

At 11:50, your instructor walks to your station. You show them:

  1. Your MVP index card. Point to each item: done or not done?
  2. Run the game from F5. They watch you play through to a loss/win and restart.
  3. Any items on the card that are not done by this point get crossed off or moved to "stretch."

After the scope check, your instructor signs your index card.

After today: no new features. Tomorrow is polish — bugs, title screen, sound, and feel. Adding new features on Day 5 is how games break right before the showcase.

Checkpoint: ✅ Game runs start to finish · ✅ Player can lose and restart · ✅ Scope card signed off by instructor.
