The node that gives a physics node its body. On its own it does nothing — it's always a child of an Area2D, CharacterBody2D, or StaticBody2D, and it tells that parent what shape it is.

⚠️The #1 mistake in the whole camp

A physics node with no CollisionShape2D, or with an empty Shape slot, detects and collides with nothing. Godot warns you with a ⚠️ triangle in the Scene dock. Click the triangle — it tells you exactly what's wrong.

#Adding one

  1. Select the Area2D / CharacterBody2D / StaticBody2D.
  2. + Add Child Node → CollisionShape2D.
  3. In the Inspector, click the Shape dropdown (it says <empty>) and pick a shape. This second step is the one everyone forgets.
  4. In the 2D viewport, drag the orange dots to resize it over your sprite.

#Which shape to pick

ShapeUse forNotes
RectangleShape2Dcrates, platforms, walls, most UI-ish triggersFastest. Default choice for anything boxy.
CircleShape2Dcoins, balls, bombs, pickupsBest for a Bouncing Ball — bounces are predictable.
CapsuleShape2Dplayers and humanoid enemiesRounded ends stop the player catching on tile seams. Use this for platformer players.
SegmentShape2Dlaser lines, thin beamsRare.
ConvexPolygonShape2Dodd rigid shapesMust be convex (no dents).
ConcavePolygonShape2Dcomplex static geometryStatic only — never put it on something that moves.

There's also CollisionPolygon2D, a separate node where you click points to draw a custom outline. Handy for a weird-shaped platform; overkill for most camp games.

#Sizing it well

The shape matters more than the sprite for how the game feels.

  • Slightly smaller than the sprite for the player's hurtbox — near misses feel fair and generous.
  • Slightly larger than the sprite for pickups — grabbing a coin feels satisfying instead of finicky.
  • Match the sprite for walls and platforms, or the world looks like it's lying to you.
💡See what you're doing

Debug → Visible Collision Shapes, then press Play. Every shape draws in cyan while the game runs. This is how you catch a shape that's the right size but offset three pixels off the sprite.

#Turning one off (safely)

You'll want this for i-frames, a dash that passes through enemies, or a door that unlocks. Never set disabled directly during a collision callback — the physics engine is mid-step and Godot will spam an error. Use set_deferred:

$Shape.set_deferred("disabled", true)    # off
$Shape.set_deferred("disabled", false)   # back on

For a Area2D you can also just turn off the whole area:

$Hurtbox.monitoring = false

#One node, many shapes

A parent can have several CollisionShape2D children and they simply add together. Useful for an L-shaped platform, or a boss made of two overlapping circles — easier than fighting with a polygon.

#The one_way_collision trick

Select a CollisionShape2D on a StaticBody2D platform → Inspector → check One Way Collision. The player can now jump up through the platform but lands on top of it. Two clicks, and your platformer instantly feels like a real one. See Platformer Physics.
