#Stand-Up + Feature Freeze Reminder

Same format as Day 4 — 60 seconds per team, Producer speaks:

  1. What state is the game in right now?
  2. What's the one most important polish item for this morning?
  3. Is there anything that might prevent a working build by 3 PM?

After each team: your instructor confirms the feature freeze. You are building nothing new today — only making what exists better or more stable.

#Polish Sprint

Work through the polish checklist in this order. Do not skip ahead.

#Polish checklist (priority order)

1. No crashes — play your game for 3 full minutes. Write down every crash or softlock. Fix those first. Nothing else matters if the game crashes in front of an audience.

2. Title screen — does your game have one? It needs:

  • Your game's name (big and readable)
  • Your team members' names
  • A "Press any key" or "Start" button that leads to the game
  • See Scene Switching for how to load scenes on button press.

3. Restart works cleanly — after losing (or winning), can the player try again? Test the full loop: start → play → lose → restart → play again. Does it work every time?

# Simplest restart button in a game-over screen:
func _on_restart_button_pressed():
	get_tree().reload_current_scene()

4. Sound — at least one of these:

  • Background music that plays during the game (see AudioStreamPlayer)
  • A sound effect when the player dies, wins, collects something, or scores
  • Both is better than one, but one is required

5. Juice (only if 1–4 are completely solid):

Pick whichever fits your game — each is a self-contained addition to a script you already have. Full code and setup steps for all four are in Juice and Game Feel:

  • Camera shake when the player is hit — a Camera2D + a short tween. Biggest wow for the least code.
  • A scale pop when a pickup is collected — the pickup grows for a split second before it queue_free()s.
  • A flashing player when in danger — a Timer toggling sprite visibility.
  • A particle burst on death — a GPUParticles2D in your main scene, triggered from game_over().
⚠️Juice is last for a reason

Juice can break your game if added quickly. If any of items 1–4 aren't solid, skip juice entirely. A stable boring game beats a sparkly crashing one.

#Other polish ideas (if you have time)

  • Add credits to your title screen or a pause screen
  • Smooth out any visual jitter in your player's movement
  • Add a high score that persists through restarts (see Score and UI)
  • Make sure all text is readable — white on light = invisible

#☕ Break

Step away for 10 minutes. Come back fresh for presentation prep.

#Presentation Prep

Your presentation will be 3–4 minutes during the showcase. Every team member has a role. Decide now who does what.

#Your presentation has four parts

Write each one on an index card:

Part 1: Game name + one-sentence pitch Say your game's name. Then say your pitch from One-Sentence Pitch. "Our game is called Lantern Run. You are a single firefly who must light 10 lanterns before dawn."

Part 2: Live demo One teammate plays the game live. One teammate narrates what's happening — point out the mechanic, the tension, the win/lose state. Don't over-explain. Let the game speak.

💡Demo tip

Play for 60–90 seconds max. Reach a game-over state on purpose if you can — it shows the full loop.

Part 3: One hard thing Pick one thing that was genuinely difficult to build — a bug that took an hour, a mechanic that required a new idea, a design decision your team disagreed on. Tell that story in 30 seconds.

Part 4: One wish If you had one more week, what's the one feature you'd add? One sentence. Make it specific — not "more levels" but "a level where the gravity flips."

#Practice your parts

  1. Each person says their part out loud — not to each other, to the room (or to a pretend audience).
  2. Set a timer for 4 minutes and do a quick run-through together. Are you over? Cut something.
  3. Decide: who holds the keyboard during the demo? Who narrates? Who presents parts 3 and 4?

#Dress Rehearsal

Full run-through for every team. Your instructor watches and gives feedback.

For each team (~5 minutes):

  1. Stand up. Introduce your team.
  2. Say your pitch.
  3. Play the demo.
  4. Tell the hard thing.
  5. Tell the wish.
  6. Your instructor gives 1 minute of feedback: what worked, what to tighten.
💡The most common feedback
  • "We couldn't see the screen well enough — can you make the window bigger?"
  • "The demo went too long — pick a shorter gameplay loop."
  • "We didn't know the game's name — say it louder and earlier."

After dress rehearsal: write down the one thing you're changing based on feedback. Then go fix it.

Checkpoint: ✅ All four presentation parts prepared · ✅ Full rehearsal completed · ✅ Team knows who does what.

#Final Polish + Load onto Demo Machine

#Get your build on the demo machine

This is the most important task of the afternoon. Do not leave it until 2:45.

  1. Export your game OR prepare to run from the editor on the showcase machine. See Exporting Your Game.
  2. If exporting: Project → Export → add a preset for your platform → Export Project. Test the exported file on the demo machine before calling it done.
  3. If running from the editor: copy your entire project folder to the showcase machine. Open it in Godot. Press F5 and make sure it runs.
  4. Play the full loop on the showcase machine once: start, play, die, restart. Confirm it works.
⚠️Export early

Export issues are the #1 last-minute crisis. Audio not loading, a missing asset, a path that only worked on your machine — these all take time to debug. Start this process by 1 PM at the latest.

#Final polish window (12:00–3:00)

While one person handles the build export, the rest of the team does final polish from the checklist. No new features. This means:

  • Bug fixes only
  • Text readability
  • Volume levels on audio
  • Making sure the title screen looks clean

#3 PM — 🚨 Hard deadline

At 3:00 PM, all changes stop. The build on the demo machine is the build. Practice your presentation one more time, then set up your station.

#Arcade Station Setup + Break

  1. Set up your arcade station:
    • Position the demo machine so the screen faces the room
    • Put a printed or handwritten name card for your game at the station
    • If you have controllers or special input, set them up and label them
  2. Break: you've been working all day. Eat something, take a breath. The showcase is yours to enjoy.
  3. Take your game home: before the showcase, email yourself the project folder, put it on a USB, or upload to itch.io. See Exporting Your Game and Where to Keep Going.
