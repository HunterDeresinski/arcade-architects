You made games this week. Here's how to keep making them after camp. It's all free.

#Keep Godot

  • Godot is free forever — download it at godotengine.org on any computer (Windows, Mac, Linux).
  • Bring your camp project home on a USB or upload it (see Exporting Your Game).

#Learn more (free tutorials)

  • Official Godot docs & tutorials — docs.godotengine.org. Start again with "Your first 2D game," then try "Your first 3D game."
  • GDQuest — gdquest.com and their YouTube — excellent beginner-to-intermediate Godot lessons.
  • Brackeys (YouTube) — friendly, popular Godot tutorial series.
  • 20 Games Challenge — 20_games_challenge — a list of classic games to rebuild in order, each one teaching new skills. Perfect next step from tiny games.

#Make more tiny games

  • Game jams — short events (a weekend to a week) where everyone makes a game on a theme. Browse itch.io/jams. Look for beginner-friendly or "no theme" jams.
  • Mini Jam / Ludum Dare / GMTK Game Jam — well-known recurring jams (some have age guidance — check, and do them with a parent).
  • Keep using constraints — give yourself "one button, one screen, one hour" and just go.

#Share what you make

  • Upload to itch.io (free) so friends and family can play in a browser (Exporting Your Game → Option C — export for the Web HTML5 and put it on itch.io).

#The habit that matters most

Finish small things. A pile of ten tiny finished games will teach you more than one giant game you never complete. That's the whole philosophy of this camp — The Tiny Game Rules.
