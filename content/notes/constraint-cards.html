🏠 Curriculum Map · Used on Day 3 - Design Day · 🖨️ Print & cut into cards.

Constraints spark ideas (see Tiny Game Design). On Day 3, each team draws one core constraint (required) and may draw one flavor constraint (optional spice). Every game must also obey The Tiny Game Rules.

#Core constraints (draw one — required)

#ConstraintMeans
1One screenThe whole game fits on one screen. No scrolling.
2One buttonThe entire game is played with a single key.
3One mechanicThe player can really only do one kind of action.
4No movementThe player can't move — the world moves around them.
5You can't winThe goal is to last as long as possible (high score).
6Two colors onlyThe whole game uses just two colors.

#Flavor constraints (optional — draw one for spice)

#FlavorMeans
ARoles reversedThe "enemy" is the goal; the "goal" hurts you.
BIt speeds upThe game gets faster the longer you survive.
COne lifeOne mistake and it's over.
DEverything fallsGravity rules; things drop from the top.
ESilent → loudStarts calm, ends chaotic.
FTiny hero, big worldYou're small; everything else is huge.

#How to use a constraint

  1. Draw your core card. Read it aloud to your team.
  2. Brainstorm: "What game could we make where [constraint]?"
  3. Don't fight the constraint — use it. "One button" isn't a problem to solve; it's the whole personality of your game (Flappy Bird is one button).
  4. Turn the best idea into a One-Sentence Pitch.
🧪Constraint → idea

One button + everything falls → "You're a bucket; tap to dash left/right and catch falling eggs before they smash." No movement + you can't win → "You're a lighthouse; rotate your beam to keep boats off the rocks as long as you can."
