Watching someone else play your game is the fastest way to make it better. They'll get stuck in ways you can't see, because you already know how it works.

#The golden rule: don't help, don't explain

When someone plays your game, stay quiet. Don't say "oh you press space" or "no, go left." Their confusion is the data. If they're lost, that's a thing to fix — not a thing to explain away. Write down where they struggle.

#How to run a playtest (Day 4 exchange)

  1. Swap machines with another team. Each plays the other's game for ~10 minutes.
  2. The makers watch silently and take notes: Where did they pause? What did they try that didn't work? Did they understand the goal?
  3. Afterward, the players give feedback in this format:

2 things that work · 1 thing that's confusing · 1 suggestion

#Giving good feedback

  • Be specific: "I didn't know I could jump" beats "it's confusing."
  • Talk about the game, not the people: "the goal wasn't clear," not "you did this wrong."
  • Lead with what works — it tells the team what to protect.

#Receiving feedback (don't get defensive)

  • You don't have to act on every note. Collect them all, then decide.
  • Sort feedback into three piles: fix now, fix if time, cut/ignore.
  • If two different testers hit the same problem, that's real — prioritize it.

#Day 2 version (lighter)

In the remix carousel, just leave one sticky note per game: ⭐ something you liked + ❓ one question. Same spirit, faster.

💡Playtest Lead's job

The Playtest Lead keeps the running bug list all week and runs the team's side of the exchange. By Friday that list becomes the polish to-do (Day 5 - Polish and Showcase).
