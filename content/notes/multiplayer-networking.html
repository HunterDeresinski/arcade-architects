Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ very hard · Camp verdict: ❌ definitely not · Do instead: Two-Player and Gamepad

#What it is

Two people on two different computers playing the same game together. Godot has real support for it — ENetMultiplayerPeer, MultiplayerSynchronizer, RPCs — and it works.

#Why definitely not

Networking is the hardest thing in this reference by a wide margin, and it's hard in a way that doesn't shrink for small games:

  • Two computers disagree about reality. Every problem downstream comes from this.
  • Latency. Your input arrives 80ms late. Do you rewind time and replay? Real games do.
  • Authority. Who decides whether the shot hit? If it's the shooter, players can cheat. If it's the server, shooting feels laggy.
  • Debugging requires two running games, two sets of logs, and bugs that only appear under real network delay.
  • School networks block things. Firewalls, client isolation on guest wifi, no port forwarding. You can lose a day to infrastructure you don't control before writing a line of game code.

Professional teams staff specialists for this. It is not a five-day-camp topic and it's not a "stretch goal" — it's a different discipline.

#The thing they actually want

Almost always: "I want to play with my friend."

That's Two-Player and Gamepad, and it's better for a camp. Two players on one keyboard or two controllers, one screen, twenty minutes of work. At the showcase, local multiplayer draws a crowd around the table in a way netplay never could — the fun of two people elbowing each other is the point.

#If they want the co-op feeling

  • Shared-screen co-op — both players in one camera. Two-Player and Gamepad has the midpoint-camera code.
  • Asymmetric co-op — one drives, one shoots. Genuinely interesting design, zero networking.
  • Hot-seat — alternate turns on one machine. Perfect for Grid-Based Movement puzzle and strategy games.
  • Async competition — a shared leaderboard. Players compete without ever being online at the same time, and this is what most arcade "multiplayer" actually was.

That last one is worth emphasizing: the original arcade multiplayer was a high score table. You never met the person who set it. You just wanted to beat them.

#If a student is determined, after camp

  • Godot docs → High-level multiplayer — start here, it's good
  • The MultiplayerSpawner / MultiplayerSynchronizer nodes handle a lot automatically
  • Steam and Nakama both offer easier hosting than raw IP connections
  • Advise building a complete single-player game first, then adding networking to it. Learning networking and game design at once is two hard things at once.

#What to say in the moment

"You want to play with your friend, right? Let's put you both on one screen — it's twenty minutes instead of a week, and honestly it's more fun at the showcase because you're sitting next to each other. Online play is a summer-long project; I can point you at where to start if you want it."
