If your team can't say your game in one sentence, it's too big (The Tiny Game Rules). Writing the sentence is the design decision. Lock it on Day 3 and get it instructor-approved.

#The template

You are a ______ who ______ until ______.

  • First blank: who/what you are (the character)
  • Second blank: what you do — the core loop verb (Tiny Game Design)
  • Third blank: how it ends — the win or lose condition

#Examples

  • "You are a raccoon who stacks trash higher and higher until it topples."
  • "You are one firefly who lights lanterns in the dark until dawn breaks."
  • "You are a bucket who catches falling eggs until three smash."
  • "You are a lighthouse who sweeps a beam to guide ships until a ship crashes."

#A stronger version (optional)

If you want more, add the feeling and the constraint:

A [one-button] game where you are a [firefly] who [lights lanterns] until [dawn] — it should feel [peaceful but tense].

#The test

Read your sentence to another team. If they can picture the game and roughly how to play it, you're ready to build. If they ask "wait, what do you actually do?", your verb is missing — fix that first.

⚠️Approval = scope check

An instructor has to approve your pitch before you build. They're checking one thing: can you finish this by Friday with Day 1–2 skills? If not, they'll help you shrink it. That's a good thing.
